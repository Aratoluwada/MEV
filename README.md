# Medicine Registry Checker

## A clearer trust signal for every medicine

In markets where counterfeit, falsified, and poorly verified medicines put lives at risk, people are often left to judge a product from its packaging or rely on information they cannot easily confirm. Medicine Registry Checker aims to put a useful, evidence-based check in the hands of the person holding the medicine.

With a photo or a few details from the package, a user can get an understandable confidence assessment: how strongly the product's visible identity aligns with available regulatory records, which details agree or conflict, and what evidence is still missing. Gemma helps interpret medicine labels and communicate the result in clear, locally accessible language. The assessment is grounded in traceable records and explicit comparisons, not an unconstrained model opinion.

## What we aim to demonstrate today

By the end of the hackathon, we aim to demonstrate a simple, compelling journey:

- A person scans or enters the details of a medicine.
- The system identifies key label information such as its name, registration number, ingredients, and strength.
- It checks those details against available regulatory information and shows the matching evidence.
- The user receives a confidence percentage, a plain-language explanation of what supports that score, and a clear next step when the evidence is incomplete or inconsistent.

The percentage is a measure of confidence in the **verification evidence available for that product**, not a clinical guarantee that a medicine is safe, effective, authentic, or appropriate for a particular person. A registry match is valuable evidence, but it cannot by itself establish product quality, storage conditions, batch integrity, or individual medical suitability. When the system cannot verify a product, it should say so plainly rather than turn missing information into an accusation.

## By the End of Today

We aim to leave the hackathon with a working demonstration of the core user promise:

- A person can scan a medicine package or enter its details.
- The system identifies the product information that can be read, including its name, registration number, ingredients, and strength.
- The system compares those details with available regulatory evidence and returns a confidence percentage with the evidence behind it.
- The result is easy to understand, with a Gemma-powered explanation and a clear next step when evidence is incomplete or inconsistent.
- When the product cannot be verified, the experience says so clearly instead of presenting uncertainty as proof of danger or safety.

## Why this can matter

The long-term opportunity is a trusted medicine-verification layer that makes reliable product information easier to access, understand, and act on. A useful first experience can grow toward broader and fresher regulatory coverage, batch and recall information, supply-chain evidence, and partnerships with health providers, pharmacies, regulators, and distributors. Each additional source can strengthen the confidence assessment and make its limits more transparent.

Our starting point is focused: turn difficult-to-read medicine packaging and hard-to-navigate records into a clear, explainable signal that helps people decide what to verify next. The product is designed to earn trust by showing its evidence and uncertainty, not by claiming certainty it cannot support.
